01 / Explore the channels

One world. Different ways to tell a story.

Each medium has unique strengths and limitations. The format affects how information reaches us, how quickly it changes, and how we respond.

📰

Print Media

Physical text and images created for reading and reference.

NewspapersBooksMagazinesFlyers

Strength: easy to annotate and revisit.

Limitation: may become outdated.

📺

Broadcast Media

Audio and video distributed through established channels.

RadioTelevisionPSAs

Strength: timely audio-visual reporting.

Limitation: editing can shape emphasis.

💻

Digital Media

Information created, stored, or shared electronically.

WebsitesEmailE-booksDatabases

Strength: searchable and easy to update.

Limitation: errors can spread quickly.

🌐

New Media

Interactive, networked media where audiences also create and respond.

Social mediaPodcastsLivestreams

Strength: participation and community voices.

Limitation: virality can amplify misinformation.

02 / Apply the ideas

Same event, different experiences.

Imagine a typhoon approaching your community. Different media can help—but they do not all play the same role.

Community scenario

A typhoon warning is issued.

Use each channel's strengths, but verify safety instructions with relevant official sources.

📰 PrintNewspaper explains preparedness steps and background.
📺 BroadcastRadio and TV share urgent updates and interviews.
💻 DigitalOfficial pages provide updated advisories and maps.
🌐 New mediaResidents share observations, questions, and local reports.

What should I trust first?

For emergency instructions, check the relevant official government agency or local government unit and confirm the advisory is current.

What should make me pause?

Undated screenshots, anonymous claims, dramatic wording, missing context, and posts asking you to share immediately.

03 / Think critically

Before you share, run the C.R.A.P. check.

A reminder to evaluate sources. No checklist guarantees truth, so corroborate important claims with independent, credible sources.

C
Currency

When was it published or updated? Is it still current?

R
Relevance

Does it answer the question I actually need answered?

A
Authority

Who created it? What expertise or evidence supports it?

P
Purpose

Is it informing, persuading, entertaining, selling, or provoking?

04 / Act responsibly

Media literacy is also an ethical choice.

Being informed means considering how our messages affect others—not only whether they get attention.

🔎

Verify first

Check the original source, date, evidence, and context. Correct mistakes when discovered.

🔐

Protect privacy

Ask for consent before posting identifiable images or private information. Review app permissions.

©️

Credit creators

Respect copyright and cite sources for images, music, video, and ideas.

💬

Communicate respectfully

Avoid cyberbullying, harassment, and harmful stereotypes.

🧠

Notice persuasion

Consider how headlines, editing, algorithms, and emotional language shape reactions.

✨

Create transparently

Use editing and AI tools honestly; do not present fabricated content as authentic evidence.

05 / Personal insight

From passive scrolling to thoughtful participation.

Media can teach us, connect communities, and give people a voice. It can also mislead us when we react without checking.

“Popularity is not proof. A responsible media user pauses, checks, and thinks before sharing.”
